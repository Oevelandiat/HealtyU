Digital Well-being

Module 5 of 5 2 subsections · 1 activity ~7 min read

Devices are part of how students study, connect and unwind — this module isn't about giving them up, but about noticing when screen time stops serving you.

Subsection 1

Screen Time

Screens aren't inherently harmful — much of academic life now runs through them — but unstructured, reactive use (checking notifications between every task) fragments attention.

Late-evening screen use, especially bright screens close to bedtime, can also delay the body's natural signal to wind down for sleep.

Noticing when screen use is intentional versus a default reflex is a more useful starting point than tracking exact hours.

Subsection 2

Digital Disconnection

Short, deliberate breaks from devices — during meals, before sleep, or during a walk — give attention a chance to reset without requiring a full digital detox.

Small structural changes, like moving a phone out of arm's reach while studying, often do more than willpower alone.

Disconnection ideas

Tap a card to see a simple way to build a short disconnection moment into your day.

Phone-free meals+

Charge outside the room+
Charging your phone outside the bedroom removes the temptation to scroll before sleep.
One walk, no headphones+
Try one short walk a week without music or a podcast — just noticing your surroundings.
